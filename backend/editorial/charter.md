# Parapet — House Editorial Charter

## Editorial Sensibility

Parapet publishes contemporary short fiction of high craft, adult intelligence,
and unsentimental observation. Our house sensibility favors work grounded in
physical reality, human friction, material stakes, and dry or stark
clarity—whether set in history, on a harsh frontier, in a plausible future, or
amid the absurdities of modern life.

## House Craft Standards

- Earn every turn through concrete action, dialogue, and physical detail—never
  explain the theme or announce a character's feelings.
- Avoid tidy epiphanies, therapeutic resolutions, and sentimental endings. Let
  pieces land with weight, irony, or quiet consequence.
- Zero AI-literary clichés: forbid phrases like "a quiet hum," "testament to,"
  "tapestry," "weight of unspoken words," "something shifted inside him," "the
  air thick with," or decorative atmosphere that does no narrative work.
- Characters must have jobs, constraints, vanities, blind spots, and believable
  incentives.
- Write in clear, accessible modern English that an attentive general reader can
  follow without a glossary. Discourage jargon—especially obscure period, trade,
  bureaucratic, or speculative sci-fi terminology, archaic equipment names, and
  invented acronyms—that a modern reader would not be likely to know. Describe
  tools, work, and future technology through plain physical language; if a
  single specialist or period term is truly indispensable, make its meaning
  unmistakable from context.

## Editorial Taste & New Genres

- **What belongs on this shelf:** Any genre or hybrid that shares the journal's
  grounded, unsentimental register—historical realism, stark regional or
  frontier fiction, plausible hard or soft sci-fi, sharp contemporary satire,
  noir, espionage, quiet weird/folk fiction, survivalist or institutional drama.
  Newcomers are encouraged to broaden the collective's range rather than
  duplicate an existing member's beat.
- **What feels out of place here:** Commercial romance or romantasy, breezy
  women's fiction, YA melodrama, cozy escapism, epic high fantasy, pulp space
  opera, tearjerkers, or didactic moralizing. Treat this as a matter of
  editorial taste rather than a bureaucratic rulebook—if a pitch feels slick,
  sentimental, or escapist, the collective will naturally turn it down.
